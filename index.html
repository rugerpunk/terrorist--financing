Built for a Solana Foundation Grant Workshop

This dashboard is part of a hands-on forensic investigation workshop held under a Solana Foundation grant. It was designed as practitioner-grade teaching material for future legal professionals who will meet on-chain evidence in courtrooms, regulators' offices and compliance teams.

PROJECT DETAILS · THE PROBLEM
Future legal professionals — prosecutors, judges, compliance officers, and regulators — graduate without any practical understanding of how blockchain ecosystems are weaponized for terrorism financing (TF) and international sanctions evasion.

This creates a critical enforcement gap: courts and regulators are confronted with on-chain evidence they cannot evaluate, and bad actors operate with near-impunity in jurisdictions where legal expertise hasn't caught up with Web3 realities.

Concrete, urgent example — the A7A5 token: a ruble-backed token documented as a mechanism for Russia to bypass OFAC sanctions through a crypto-energy transfer network involving Kyrgyz, Chinese, and Hong Kong nodes.

Without accessible, practitioner-grade educational infrastructure on Solana's forensic landscape, the next generation of law professionals will remain blind to these schemes. It is imperative for us, experts in our field, to contribute and share our expertise in the best way possible — and hands-on workshops are the best way to impact the community.

Overview & Definitions

Terrorist financing (TF) through virtual assets represents one of the most complex investigative challenges in modern crypto forensics. Unlike traditional money laundering, TF flows may involve small amounts from legitimate sources, making pattern recognition critical.

Key Distinction: TF funds may originate from clean sources — detection relies on destination and behavioral patterns, not just origin tracing.
VASPs are legally required to file Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) with FIUs when TF indicators are detected.
☣️
TERRORIST FINANCING

The provision or collection of funds — from any source, clean or illicit — with the intention that they be used to support terrorist acts, organizations, or individual terrorists. Virtual assets enable rapid, borderless, pseudonymous transfers.

🔍
VIRTUAL ASSET (VA)

A digital representation of value that can be digitally traded, transferred, or used for payment. Includes cryptocurrencies (BTC, ETH, Monero), stablecoins, and tokens — all exploitable for TF layering.

🏦
VASP

Virtual Asset Service Provider — exchanges, custodians, wallet providers. Primary AML/CTF gatekeepers. TF actors exploit VASPs in jurisdictions with weak KYC controls as relay points.

📋
STR / SAR

Suspicious Transaction Report — the primary intelligence product filed by VASPs and FIs with Financial Intelligence Units when TF red flags are identified. Triggers LEA investigation.

Why Terrorist Funding in Crypto Matters to Forensic Investigators

The unique technical properties of virtual assets create both opportunity and hazard. Understanding why VAs are attractive for terrorist financing is the foundation of effective detection.

🌐
Borderless Speed

TF actors can transfer funds across international jurisdictions in seconds — outpacing traditional SWIFT monitoring and correspondent banking controls. A transaction from Europe to Southeast Asia settles in minutes, not days.

Rapid settlement Cross-border No intermediary
🕵️
Pseudonymity Exploitation

Blockchain addresses provide pseudonymity rather than true anonymity — but layered through mixers, privacy coins, and multiple wallets, effective anonymity is achievable. LEAs must apply clustering analysis to pierce this veil.

Wallet clustering Chain analysis AEC coins
💸
Small-Amount Fundraising

TF via crypto often involves aggregation of many small donations — well below reporting thresholds — from sympathizers in multiple jurisdictions. A terrorist operation may be funded by hundreds of micro-transactions undetectable individually.

Structuring Crowdfunding Smurfing
⚖️
Regulatory Arbitrage

TF actors deliberately route funds through VASPs operating in jurisdictions where FATF standards are not yet implemented. Forensic investigators must map the full jurisdictional chain to identify weak links and alert partner FIUs.

Weak-KYC VASPs FIU cooperation FATF gaps
🛒
Darknet Weapons Procurement

VAs are the primary payment mechanism on darknet markets for firearms, explosives, and CBRN precursors. Blockchain forensics can link wallet addresses to darknet market participation — a critical TF investigation pathway.

Darknet markets TOR nodes Weapons finance
✈️
Foreign Terrorist Fighter Financing

VAs are used to fund travel, logistics, and operational costs of Foreign Terrorist Fighters (FTFs). Investigation requires identifying VA-to-fiat cash-out points in conflict-adjacent jurisdictions and correlating with travel intelligence.

FTF tracking Travel funding Cross-intel

Red Flag Indicators — TF Focus

Six FATF categories of red flag indicators, analyzed through a terrorist financing forensic lens.

T-01Structuring under thresholds
VA transactions in small amounts specifically designed to avoid record-keeping thresholds — mirrors cash smurfing. In TF context, look for geographic clusters aligning with known extremist networks.
T-02Multiple high-value transfers in 24h
Staggered, regular-pattern high-value transactions with long dormancy periods — ransomware signature, but also TF operational funding pattern. Timestamp correlation critical.
T-03Immediate transfer to weak-KYC VASPs
Immediate forwarding of funds to VASPs in non-FATF-compliant jurisdictions after deposit — primary TF conduit technique. Map full jurisdictional chain.
T-04VA→VASP→immediate withdrawal
Using a VASP purely as a money transmission relay — depositing then immediately withdrawing without any exchange activity. Turns legitimate VASP into effective TF mixer.
T-05Unnecessary multi-VA conversion
Converting BTC→ETH→XMR without logical portfolio rationale. Each conversion creates a new forensic trace point — but also adds obfuscation layers. Document conversion chain.
T-06Stolen fund deposit (ZA case)
USD 108M case (South Africa): mule networks depositing at multiple FIs, then purchasing VA for immediate foreign transfer. Money mule indicators: shared IP/address patterns.
P-01Large initial deposit → full same-day trade
New VASP account with large opening deposit immediately traded in full — inconsistent with legitimate investment behavior. In TF, funds are moved rapidly before detection.
P-02Multiple accounts, same IP address
Frequent transactions to same VA account from same IP — potential mule network coordination. Cross-reference IP with known TF infrastructure nodes.
P-03Accumulation from unrelated wallets
Many small incoming transfers from unrelated wallets, consolidated then forwarded — classic TF crowdfunding aggregation. Graph node analysis essential.
P-04Exchange at operational loss
Conducting VA-fiat conversion at a calculated loss, ignoring abnormally high commission fees. TF actors prioritize speed/anonymity over profitability — a key behavioral differentiator.
P-05Recurrent time-pattern transfers
Cayman Islands case: two transfers of USD 4.8M six minutes apart. Recurrent, time-bound patterns may indicate automated TF payment schedules or operational funding cycles.
P-06New user — entire balance withdrawal
New account immediately attempting to withdraw or trade 100% of deposited balance — particularly suspicious when coupled with inconsistent KYC profile.
A-01Privacy coin / AEC usage
Transaction in Monero (XMR), Zcash (ZEC), Dash, or Grin — especially when converting from transparent BTC on a VASP then immediately withdrawing. Highest TF anonymity risk indicator.
A-02TOR / VPN / darknet access
Users accessing VASP platform via Tor exit nodes, known VPN IP ranges, or proxied domain registrations. AlphaBay case: TOR infrastructure concealed USD 1B+ in VA transactions.
A-03Mixing/tumbling service exposure
Helix case: 350,000 BTC tumbled (~USD 300M). Any wallet with direct/indirect mixer exposure should be scored HIGH risk. Apply FATF travel rule compliance checks.
A-04P2P unregistered VASP activity
Operating as unlicensed P2P exchanger — charging premium fees, handling large VA volumes on behalf of third parties. Bypasses all AML/CTF controls entirely.
A-05Multiple wallets — single IP control
Large number of apparently unrelated wallets controlled from same IP/MAC address — shell wallet networks used to conceal relationship between TF participants and coordinators.
A-06Hardware/paper wallet border transit
Physical transport of VA across borders via hardware wallets — bypasses traditional currency declaration requirements. Correlate with border crossing intelligence and travel data.
I-01Sanctioned jurisdiction IP origins
Transactions initiated from IP addresses in OFAC/UN sanctioned jurisdictions, or from IPs previously flagged in other STRs. Immediate escalation required — potential TF network node.
I-02Forged KYC documents
USD 91M case: doctored IDs used to circumvent KYC at multiple exchanges. TF actors invest in sophisticated document fraud — use biometric verification and liveness detection.
I-03VA address on criminal forums
Customer's wallet address appearing on public darknet forums, extremist fundraising threads, or ransom demand communications. Cross-reference with Chainalysis/Elliptic databases.
I-04Technology-naïve elderly users
Finland investment scam case: pensioners used as unwitting mules. In TF context, financially vulnerable individuals recruited as conduits. Identify for victim protection AND TF network mapping.
I-05Illicit message field language
Transaction memo fields containing coded references to weapons, substances, or extremist terminology. Parse memo fields systematically — critical OSINT source often overlooked.
I-06Repeated multi-IP login patterns
Customer logging into VASP from multiple distinct IP addresses per day — possible account takeover OR multiple-operator TF cell sharing a single account for fund movements.
S-01Sanctioned address exposure
Wallet transacting with OFAC-listed addresses or wallets linked to UN-sanctioned entities. Instant STR obligation. Use blockchain analytics to detect even indirect (2nd/3rd hop) exposure.
S-02Darknet marketplace origin
Funds traceable to darknet market proceeds — even post-mixer. AlphaBay/DeepDotWeb cases demonstrate how FBI/DEA traced VA through mixer layers to identify TF beneficiaries.
S-03Crypto-to-plastic (prepaid cards)
USD 35M in stolen VAs converted to prepaid debit cards — cash-equivalent instruments that bypass banking monitoring. Flag accounts using multiple debit cards linked to VA wallets.
S-04Shell company / ICO obscuration
DeepDotWeb case: USD 15M in kickback BTC laundered through shell company bank accounts. In TF, ICOs serve as plausible-deniability fundraising vehicles — no investor identity required.
S-05Online gambling channel
VA transactions routed through online gambling platforms — chip-dumping or wash trading used to generate clean withdrawal records. A TF integration technique increasingly observed by FIUs.
S-06Third-party mixer sourced funds
Customer's fund source is directly traced to a mixing service output wallet. No legitimate business explanation exists for this. Immediate STR + forensic wallet clustering warranted.
G-01Non-resident VASP usage
Customer using a VASP registered in a different jurisdiction than where they reside or conduct business — without clear rationale. TF actors shop for weakest regulatory environment.
G-02Zero-regulation jurisdiction routing
The "VASP migration" pattern: exchanges relocating operations from regulated jurisdictions (KR, JP, EU) to zero-regulation territories to escape AML controls. Document jurisdictional hop chain.
G-03Unlicensed Bitcoin dealer (US case)
USD 3.29M in Bitcoin purchases through an Asian exchange after US account closure for suspicious activity. Unlicensed VA dealers are critical TF conduits — target for undercover LEA operations.
G-04Conflict-zone VASP destination
Funds flowing toward VASPs or P2P contacts in conflict-affected regions with weak state authority over financial supervision. Correlate with FTF travel intelligence from national security agencies.
G-05Business offices in no-regulation zones
Customer establishing or relocating business offices to jurisdictions without VA regulation — without clear commercial rationale. Structural red flag for deliberate AML/CTF evasion architecture.
G-06Cross-border precious metals link
USD case: Bitcoin proceeds exchanged for precious metals held in a neighboring country, imported below reporting thresholds. Multi-asset TF layering — requires cross-agency cooperation.

Investigation Methodology

A structured 5-phase forensic investigation framework for TF cases involving virtual assets, integrating FATF red flag analysis with operational blockchain intelligence.

Investigator's Note: TF investigations differ from ML in that the destination of funds is the primary concern. Always trace forward — not just backward.
INVESTIGATION COMPLETION STAGES
Intelligence CollectionPhase 1
On-chain TracingPhase 2
Entity AttributionPhase 3
Legal Process / MLAPhase 4
Asset Seizure & STR FilingPhase 5
01
PHASE 01 — INTELLIGENCE COLLECTION
STR Triage & OSINT Pre-analysis
Receive and triage Suspicious Transaction Reports from VASPs and FIs. Cross-reference reported wallet addresses against known TF databases (OFAC SDN, UN Consolidated List, Chainalysis KYTE, Elliptic). Conduct OSINT sweep of customer profiles, social media, and darknet forum mentions. Establish preliminary TF hypothesis based on FATF red flag category mapping.
02
PHASE 02 — ON-CHAIN FORENSIC TRACING
Blockchain Transaction Graph Analysis
Deploy blockchain analytics tools (Chainalysis Reactor, TRM Labs, Crystal) to map the full transaction graph. Apply wallet clustering algorithms (common-input ownership heuristic, change address detection) to deanonymize entity relationships. Trace through mixing services using timing analysis and amount correlation. Document all hops with timestamps — forward trace to identify TF deployment addresses.
03
PHASE 03 — ENTITY ATTRIBUTION
VASP Data Requests & KYC Cross-match
Issue formal data requests to VASPs holding custody of identified wallets. Cross-match KYC data against watchlists, biometric databases, and document authenticity checks. Correlate IP addresses from VASP logs with VPN/TOR exit nodes, geolocation data, and device fingerprints. Request IP logs from ISPs via legal process where applicable. Identify money mule networks through shared biographic indicators.
04
PHASE 04 — INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION
FIU/LEA Deconfliction & MLA Process
Share intelligence packages with partner FIUs via Egmont Group secure communications. Initiate Mutual Legal Assistance (MLA) requests for evidence from foreign jurisdictions. Deconflict with parallel investigations to avoid compromising ongoing intelligence operations. Coordinate with Europol, Interpol, and FinCEN for cross-border TF network takedowns. Apply UN Security Council TF-related asset freeze obligations.
05
PHASE 05 — ENFORCEMENT & REPORTING
Asset Seizure, Prosecution Package & VASP STR Feedback
Execute coordinated asset freezes across jurisdictions. Compile forensic evidence package for prosecution — blockchain evidence, expert witness statements, chain-of-custody documentation. Provide TF typology feedback to originating VASPs to enhance their future detection capability. File final analytical report with national FIU for red flag database update. Publish typology guidance (de-identified) for sector-wide AML/CTF improvement.

Forensic Case Studies

Three reconstructed investigation scenarios derived from FATF-reported cases, presented through a TF forensic analysis lens.

CASE STUDY // 01 · SOUTH AFRICA / SOUTH KOREA
Mule Network VA Laundering — USD 108M
🇿🇦 South Africa · 🇰🇷 South Korea
150+ money mules purchased large VA amounts and immediately transferred to overseas VASPs. All accessed from shared IP addresses — indicating professional money launderer coordination. Korean variant: 55 transfers across 48 accounts to obscure KRW 400M phishing proceeds.

TF Forensic Lens: Mule network infrastructure mirrors TF operational security protocols. Shared IP clustering is the critical detection point — flag all accounts originating from same IP block. STR filing triggered successful fund seizure in Korean case.
Money mules IP clustering Multi-VASP transfer STR success
CASE STUDY // 02 · UNITED STATES
AlphaBay Darknet Takedown — USD 1B+
🇺🇸 United States · Multi-jurisdictional
AlphaBay operated on TOR with 200,000 users, 40,000 vendors, facilitating over USD 1B in VA transactions for drugs, weapons, malware, and stolen documents 2015–2017. Funds traced through VA transaction graph from AlphaBay wallets to exchange accounts, then to bank accounts and physical assets.

TF Forensic Lens: Darknet marketplaces are primary weapons procurement channels for terrorist groups. VA tracing through TOR-originated wallets requires cooperation with Tor Project data and exchange subpoenas. The administrator's physical assets (USD-denominated) were the key attribution vector.
Darknet market TOR infrastructure Weapons trafficking VA tracing
CASE STUDY // 03 · FINLAND / ITALY
Investment Scam TF Mule Network — Pensioner Targeting
🇫🇮 Finland · 🇮🇹 Italy · Multi-continent
Foreign nationals targeted elderly pensioners via phone/email/social media offering fake VA investment opportunities. Victims used as unwitting TF mules — instructed to deposit funds via crypto ATMs to attacker-specified wallet addresses. Remote desktop access used to conduct unauthorized transfers.

TF Forensic Lens: Victim-mule network maps to foreign terrorist fighter funding architecture. Crypto ATM transactions are forensically rich — log IP, device, facial recognition data. Prosecution requires victim support framework. Italian variant: EUR 180K dark trading linked to darknet markets, online betting, and mixing — textbook TF integration.
Social engineering Crypto ATM Victim protection Remote access

WALLET CLUSTERING TECHNIQUES
Common-Input-Ownership Heuristic
Multiple inputs in a single transaction likely controlled by same entity. Build cluster graphs to identify controlling wallets in TF networks.
Change Address Detection
Identify unspent change outputs returning to sender. Trace TF operational wallets through repeated change address patterns.
Timing & Amount Correlation
Post-mixer: correlate amounts and timestamps of input/output transactions to pierce mixing obfuscation. Effective against basic tumblers.
ADVANCED FORENSIC METHODOLOGIES
Graph Neural Network Analysis
ML-based transaction graph classification (Elliptic dataset trained models) — automated TF cluster identification at scale across millions of transactions.
Cross-chain Transaction Tracing
Following funds through bridge protocols and atomic swaps between chains. TF actors use cross-chain movement to exploit per-chain analytics tool limitations.
UTXO Set Analysis
For Bitcoin: analyze unspent transaction output sets to identify dormant TF fund reserves. Cross-reference with threat intelligence timelines for activation patterns.
